1. PHARYNGEAL ARCHES & POUCHES (Embryology)

The pharyngeal (branchial) arches are paired structures that
appear in the 4th–5th week of development. Each arch has a core of mesenchyme, a cartilaginous rod, an aortic arch artery, a nerve, and muscles.

Mnemonic for Pouch Derivatives: "Every Teacher Teaches In School Carefully"

  • Every → Ear (Middle ear cavity) - 1st Pouch
  • Teacher → Tonsil (Palatine) - 2nd Pouch
  • Teaches → Thymus - 3rd Pouch (ventral wing)
  • In → Inferior parathyroid - 3rd Pouch (dorsal wing)
  • School → Superior parathyroid - 4th Pouch (dorsal wing)
  • Carefully → C cells (Parafollicular) - 4th Pouch (ventral wing)

DiGeorge Syndrome (22q11.2 Deletion):

  • Gene involved: TBX1 — a key transcription factor for pharyngeal arch development
  • Classic Triad: Cardiac anomalies (conotruncal), Hypoplastic thymus (T-cell immunodeficiency), Hypocalcemia (parathyroid hypoplasia)
  • Mnemonic: CATCH-22(Cardiac defects, Abnormal facies, Thymic hypoplasia, Cleft palate, Hypocalcemia, chromosome 22)
  • Most common microdeletion syndrome in humans (~1:4000 live births)

Clinical Correlate — Branchial Cysts/Fistulas:

  • Cervical sinusis formed by 2nd–4th grooves being overgrown by the 2nd arch.
    Failure of obliteration → Branchial cyst/fistula
  • Location: Anterior to sternocleidomastoid (SCM)

Neural Crest Cells (NCCs):

  • Derived from rhombomeres R1, R2 (1st arch), R4 (2nd arch), R6/R7 (3rd arch), R8 (4th & 6th arches)
  • R3 and R5 contribute few cells; most undergo apoptosis
  • NCCs are pivotal for craniofacial skeletal and connective tissues; defects cause
    DiGeorge or Treacher Collins syndromes
