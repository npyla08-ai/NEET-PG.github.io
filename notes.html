1. Pharyngeal Arches & Pouches

The pharyngeal (branchial) arches are paired structures that appear in the 4th–5th week of development. Each arch has a core of mesenchyme, a cartilaginous rod, an aortic arch artery, a nerve, and muscles.

The Five Arches at a Glance

  • 1st Arch (Mandibular) — Nerve: Trigeminal (CN V)
  • 2nd Arch (Hyoid) — Nerve: Facial (CN VII)
  • 3rd Arch — Nerve: Glossopharyngeal (CN IX)
  • 4th Arch — Nerve: Vagus (CN X) — Superior laryngeal branch
  • 6th Arch — Nerve: Vagus (CN X) — Recurrent laryngeal branch

1st Arch (Mandibular) — Full Details

  • Nerve: Trigeminal (CN V)
  • Muscles: Muscles of mastication, Mylohyoid, Anterior belly of digastric, Tensor tympani, Tensor veli palatini
  • Skeletal / Cartilage: Malleus, Incus, Mandible (Meckel's cartilage), Maxilla, Zygomatic bone, Sphenomandibular ligament

2nd Arch (Hyoid) — Full Details

  • Nerve: Facial (CN VII)
  • Muscles: Muscles of facial expression, Stapedius, Stylohyoid, Posterior belly of digastric, Buccinator, Platysma
  • Skeletal / Cartilage: Stapes, Styloid process, Lesser horn of hyoid (Reichert's cartilage), Stylohyoid ligament

3rd Arch — Full Details

  • Nerve: Glossopharyngeal (CN IX)
  • Muscles: Stylopharyngeus (the only muscle)
  • Skeletal / Cartilage: Greater horn and body of hyoid

4th Arch — Full Details

  • Nerve: Vagus (CN X) — Superior laryngeal branch
  • Muscles: Pharyngeal constrictors, Cricothyroid, Levator palatini
  • Skeletal / Cartilage: Thyroid cartilage, Epiglottis, Superior laryngeal cartilages

6th Arch — Full Details

  • Nerve: Vagus (CN X) — Recurrent laryngeal branch
  • Muscles: All intrinsic laryngeal muscles EXCEPT Cricothyroid
  • Skeletal / Cartilage: Cricoid, Arytenoids, Inferior laryngeal cartilages

Pharyngeal Pouch Derivatives

  • 1st Pouch: Middle ear cavity, Eustachian tube, Mastoid antrum
  • 2nd Pouch: Palatine tonsil crypts, Tonsillar fossa
  • 3rd Pouch: Inferior parathyroid gland (dorsal wing), Thymus (ventral wing)
  • 4th Pouch: Superior parathyroid gland (dorsal wing), Ultimobranchial body → Parafollicular C cells (ventral wing)
🧠 Mnemonic — Pouch Derivatives:
"Every Teacher Teaches In School Carefully"
Every → Ear (Middle ear cavity) — 1st Pouch
Teacher → Tonsil (Palatine) — 2nd Pouch
Teaches → Thymus — 3rd Pouch (ventral wing)
In → Inferior parathyroid — 3rd Pouch (dorsal wing)
School → Superior parathyroid — 4th Pouch (dorsal wing)
Carefully → C cells (Parafollicular) — 4th Pouch (ventral wing)

Neural Crest Cells (NCCs)

  • Derived from rhombomeres R1, R2 (1st arch), R4 (2nd arch), R6/R7 (3rd arch), R8 (4th and 6th arches).
  • R3 and R5 contribute few cells — most undergo apoptosis.
  • NCCs are pivotal for craniofacial skeletal and connective tissues; defects cause DiGeorge or Treacher Collins syndromes.
🩺 Clinical Correlate — Branchial Cysts/Fistulas:
The cervical sinus is formed by the 2nd–4th grooves being overgrown by the 2nd arch. Failure of obliteration → branchial cyst / fistula. Location: Anterior to sternocleidomastoid (SCM).

2. DiGeorge Syndrome (22q11.2 Deletion)

Most common microdeletion syndrome in humans (~1:4000 live births).

  • Gene involved: TBX1 — a key transcription factor for pharyngeal arch development.
  • Chromosome: 22q11.2 (microdeletion).
  • Cardiac: Conotruncal anomalies — Tetralogy of Fallot, Truncus arteriosus, VSD.
  • Thymus: Hypoplastic → T-cell immunodeficiency.
  • Parathyroid: Hypoplastic → Hypocalcemia → tetany, seizures.
  • Face: Cleft palate, abnormal facies, low-set ears.
🧠 Mnemonic — CATCH-22:
Cardiac defects • Abnormal facies • Thymic hypoplasia • Cleft palate • Hypocalcemia • chromosome 22

3. Urinary System Development

Two Primordia of the Kidney

  • Ureteric Bud (Metanephric diverticulum): Outgrowth from the caudal mesonephric duct. Gives rise to the collecting system — Ureter, Renal pelvis, Major and minor calyces, Collecting ducts.
  • Metanephric Blastema (Metanephric mass of mesenchyme): Caudal part of the nephrogenic cord. Gives rise to the nephron — Glomerulus, PCT, Loop of Henle, DCT.
  • Reciprocal induction between these two is essential for kidney development.
Fig 1. Ureteric bud induces metanephric blastema → kidney forms.

Bladder & Urethra Derivatives

  • Bladder (main part): Vesical part of urogenital sinus.
  • Trigone of bladder: Distal portions of mesonephric ducts.
  • Urachus: Allantois → Median umbilical ligament.
  • Urethra (proximal): Pelvic part of urogenital sinus.
  • Prostate: Urogenital sinus endoderm.
  • Membranous & penile urethra: Phallic part of urogenital sinus.

Ascent of the Kidney

  • Starts in pelvis → ascends to abdomen by 9th week.
  • Rotates 90° — hilum moves from ventral to anteromedial.
  • Blood supply changes: initially from common iliac → later from abdominal aorta (renal arteries).
🩺 Clinical Correlates:
  • Bilateral renal agenesis → Oligohydramnios → Potter's syndrome.
  • Horseshoe kidney → Fusion of lower poles; arrested ascent at inferior mesenteric artery.
  • Duplication of ureter → Incomplete / complete division of ureteric bud.
  • Urachal fistula / cyst / sinus → Failure of urachus obliteration.

4. Dermatomes

A dermatome is an area of skin supplied by a single spinal nerve. Dermatome maps are essential for localizing spinal cord lesions.

Key Dermatome Landmarks (Upper Limb)

  • C5: Lateral antecubital fossa, lateral arm (deltoid patch)
  • C6: Thumb, lateral forearm, lateral hand
  • C7: Middle finger
  • C8: Little finger, medial hand
  • T1: Medial antecubital fossa, medial forearm
  • T2: Apex of axilla, medial upper arm

Key Dermatome Landmarks (Trunk)

  • T3: Nipples (T4 in some texts)
  • T6: Xiphoid process
  • T10: Umbilicus
  • T12: Inguinal / suprapubic area

Key Dermatome Landmarks (Lower Limb)

  • L2: Mid-anterior thigh
  • L3: Knee
  • L4: Medial aspect of calf, medial malleolus
  • L5: Dorsum of foot, great toe
  • S1: Sole, lateral foot, little toe, Achilles tendon
🧠 Mnemonic:
T4 = Teats (nipple line) • T10 = Tummy (umbilicus) • L4 = Leg (medial calf, crosses patella)
🩺 Clinical Pearl: In angina / MI, pain radiates to T1–T4 dermatomes (medial arm, chest, jaw). This follows the Foerster map — the medial upper limb is innervated by T1–T3.

5. Histology — Respiratory & Urinary Tracts

Respiratory Tract Epithelium

  • Nasal cavity, Trachea, Primary bronchi: Ciliated pseudostratified columnar with goblet cells.
  • Secondary / Tertiary bronchi: Ciliated pseudostratified columnar (decreasing goblet cells).
  • Bronchioles: Ciliated simple columnar → Club (Clara) cells appear.
  • Terminal bronchioles: Ciliated simple columnar with Club cells.
  • Respiratory bronchioles: Simple cuboidal (non-ciliated).
  • Alveoli: Simple squamous (Type I pneumocytes) + Type II pneumocytes (surfactant).
📌 Key pattern down the bronchial tree:
Epithelium height decreases • Goblet cells decrease • Glands decrease • Cartilage decreases • Smooth muscle increases • Elastic fibers increase.

Urinary Tract Epithelium

  • PCT, DCT: Simple cuboidal (PCT has brush border).
  • Collecting duct: Simple cuboidal → columnar.
  • Calyces, Ureter, Bladder: Transitional epithelium (Urothelium).
  • Proximal urethra: Transitional epithelium.
  • Distal urethra: Stratified squamous.

6. Joints (Arthrology)

Six Types of Synovial Joints

  • Ball & Socket (Multi-axial): Hip, Shoulder.
  • Saddle (Biaxial): 1st Carpometacarpal (thumb), Calcaneocuboid, Incudostapedial.
  • Condylar / Ellipsoid (Biaxial): Atlanto-occipital, TMJ, Knee (modified).
  • Pivot (Uniaxial): Atlanto-axial, Proximal radioulnar, Incudomalleolar.
  • Hinge (Uniaxial): Elbow, Knee, Ankle, Interphalangeal.
  • Plane / Gliding (Non-axial): Acromioclavicular, Costotransverse, Intercarpal.

Winging of Scapula

  • Medial winging: Long thoracic nerve (C5,6,7) → Serratus anterior affected.
  • Lateral winging: Spinal accessory nerve (CN XI) → Trapezius affected.
  • Lateral winging: Dorsal scapular nerve (C5) → Rhomboids affected.

Spinal Cord Anatomy

  • Lower border of spinal cord (adult): L1.
  • Lower border of spinal cord (newborn): L3.
  • Pia mater: Extends to coccyx.
  • Arachnoid mater: Ends at S2 (lower border).
  • Subarachnoid space: Extends to S2.

7. Composite Muscles

Definition: Muscles with more than one set of fibers performing the same function, usually supplied by different nerves for different parts.

Complete List of Composite Muscles

  • Digastric: Anterior belly — Nerve to mylohyoid; Posterior belly — Facial nerve (CN VII).
  • Flexor Digitorum Profundus: Medial part — Ulnar nerve; Lateral part — Anterior interosseous nerve.
  • Adductor Magnus: Adductor part — Obturator nerve; Hamstring part — Tibial nerve (sciatic).
  • Biceps Femoris: Long head — Tibial nerve (sciatic); Short head — Common peroneal nerve.
  • Pectineus: Anterior fibers — Femoral nerve; Posterior fibers — Obturator nerve.
  • Brachialis: Proprioception — Radial nerve; Motor — Musculocutaneous nerve.
  • Flexor Pollicis Brevis: Superficial head — Median nerve; Deep head — Deep branch of ulnar nerve.
  • Pectoralis Major: Medial and lateral pectoral nerves.
  • Subscapularis: Upper and lower subscapular nerves.
⚠️ NEET PG Favourite: "All are composite muscles EXCEPT" — Rectus femoris is a distractor (it is NOT a composite muscle — single nerve supply: femoral nerve).

Other High-Yield Muscle Facts

  • Smallest muscle: Stapedius
  • Longest muscle: Sartorius
  • Most common congenitally absent muscle: Pectoralis major

8. Perineal Pouches

Superficial Perineal Pouch — Contents

  • Muscles: Ischiocavernosus, Bulbospongiosus, Superficial transverse perineal muscle.
  • Erectile tissues: Crura of penis / clitoris, Bulb of penis / vestibule.
  • Glands: Greater vestibular (Bartholin's) glands (female).
  • Vessels / Nerves: Branches of internal pudendal artery and pudendal nerve.

Deep Perineal Pouch — Contents

  • Membranous urethra (male) / Urethra + Vagina (female).
  • External urethral sphincter (Sphincter urethrae).
  • Deep transverse perineal muscle.
  • Bulbourethral (Cowper's) glands (male).
  • Vessels / Nerves: Dorsal nerve and artery of penis / clitoris.
🧠 Mnemonic — Superficial Pouch:
"Some Boys Prefer Sex"
Some → Ischiocavernosus • Boys → Bulbospongiosus • Prefer → Perineal (superficial transverse) • Sex → Structures (crura, bulb, Bartholin's glands)
🩺 Clinical Anatomy:
  • Perineal body — central tendon of perineum; point of convergence of bulbospongiosus, external anal sphincter, levator ani, deep and superficial transverse perineal muscles.
  • Episiotomy — incision in perineum to prevent perineal tear during childbirth.

9. Quick Revision Summary

Embryology — Must-Know

  • Pharyngeal Arches: 1st → Trigeminal (V); 2nd → Facial (VII); 3rd → Glossopharyngeal (IX); 4th → Vagus (Superior Laryngeal); 6th → Vagus (Recurrent Laryngeal).
  • Pharyngeal Pouches: 3rd → Thymus + Inferior parathyroid; 4th → Superior parathyroid + C cells.
  • DiGeorge: 22q11.2 deletion, TBX1 gene, CATCH-22, Thymic aplasia + Hypocalcemia + Cardiac defects.
  • Urinary Embryology: Ureteric bud → Collecting system; Metanephric blastema → Nephron; Trigone ← Mesonephric duct.

Dermatomes — Must-Know

  • T4 = Nipple • T10 = Umbilicus • L4 = Medial calf • L5 = Great toe • S1 = Lateral foot.

Histology — Must-Know

  • Respiratory: Trachea = Pseudostratified ciliated columnar; Alveoli = Simple squamous.
  • Urinary: Bladder / Ureter = Transitional (Umbrella cells).

Osteology / Arthrology — Must-Know

  • Ball & Socket = Hip / Shoulder; Saddle = 1st CMC; Pivot = Atlanto-axial.

Myology — Must-Know

  • Composite Muscles: Digastric, Biceps femoris, Adductor magnus, Pectineus, FDP, FPB, Brachialis.

Perineal Anatomy — Must-Know

  • Superficial pouch: Ischiocavernosus, Bulbospongiosus, Superficial transverse perineal.
  • Deep pouch: Sphincter urethrae, Deep transverse perineal, Cowper's glands.
